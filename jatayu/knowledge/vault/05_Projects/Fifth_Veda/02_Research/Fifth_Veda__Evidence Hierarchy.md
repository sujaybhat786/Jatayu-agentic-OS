# Evidence Hierarchy

## Levels of Evidence
Sources are rigorously ranked[cite: 6]. Lower-ranked sources may only support a claim, never establish it on their own[cite: 6].

*   **Tier 1:** Original texts (Sanskrit, Pali, Tamil, or other primary-language sources) and official archaeological or governmental records[cite: 6].
*   **Tier 2:** Peer-reviewed academic translations and journal publications[cite: 6].
*   **Tier 3:** University press books and credentialed-author non-fiction with a documented citation trail[cite: 6].
*   **Tier 4:** Reputable journalism and secondary reporting. Used only to corroborate a Tier 1–3 claim, never as the sole source[cite: 6].

*Note: General encyclopedic sources, forum content, and unsourced video content are never treated as sources at any tier[cite: 6].*

## Priority Order & Conflict Resolution
Within a research package, evidence is ranked by how directly it establishes the claim[cite: 6]. 
*   A specific verse number outranks a paraphrase[cite: 6].
*   A peer-reviewed dating outranks a popular-history estimate[cite: 6].
*   A named archaeological report outranks a general historical consensus statement[cite: 6].

## When Evidence is Insufficient
If a topic requires a minimum threshold of independent peer-reviewed or academically credentialed support and that threshold cannot be met, the topic must be reframed to a narrower, defensible claim, or shelved[cite: 6]. There is no volume of popular-source corroboration that can substitute for a missing peer-reviewed foundation[cite: 6].
