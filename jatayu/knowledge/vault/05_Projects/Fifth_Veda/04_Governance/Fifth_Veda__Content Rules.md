# Content Rules

## Non-Negotiable Content Rules
*   **No Claim Without Source:** No claim may be made without a traceable source, in any content, at any duration, without exception[cite: 18].
*   **No Hedging:** No sentence may begin with a hedging or filler transitional construction (e.g., "It is important to note")[cite: 18].
*   **Specific Attribution:** No unattributed collective framing (e.g., 'ancient Indian scholars') may be used where a named historical individual is available[cite: 18].
*   **Silent CTA:** No spoken call-to-action may be appended after the closing statement; the CTA is strictly silent and on-screen only[cite: 18].
*   **Visual Integrity:** No visual anti-pattern (such as cartoon aesthetics or tourist gloss) is permitted in any published shot[cite: 18].

## Prohibited Practices
The following are strictly banned from production under all circumstances:
*   **Domestic Politics:** Content regarding current Indian domestic politics, under any framing[cite: 18].
*   **Contempt:** Content that expresses contempt for another civilisation, religion, or people as a device for elevating Indian civilisation[cite: 18].
*   **Pseudoscience:** A scientific or historical claim with no peer-reviewed or primary-source backing, presented as settled fact[cite: 18].
*   **Out-of-Context Quoting:** A quotation used outside the documented context in which it was originally made[cite: 18].
*   **AI Likenesses:** An AI-generated likeness used to depict a real, named historical figure[cite: 18].

## Evidence-First Policy
Evidence must be gathered and evaluated before a narrative frame is chosen, never the reverse[cite: 18]. A topic must be reframed or narrowed to match what the evidence actually supports; evidence is never selectively gathered to fit a predetermined narrative frame[cite: 18].
