# Editorial Standards

## Editorial Philosophy
The tone must remain precise, declarative, and unhurried in its confidence[cite: 18]. The narrative states conclusions once evidence has been laid, rather than hedging toward them[cite: 18]. 

## Political Neutrality Policy
*   **Historical Framing:** Historical evidence—including evidence of colonial-era extraction, destruction, or policy—must be presented strictly as historical evidence, sourced and documented[cite: 18].
*   **No Present-Day Extrapolation:** It is never extended into commentary on current-day domestic politics, in India or elsewhere[cite: 18].
*   **Heightened Scrutiny:** Content addressing the colonial period is held to a heightened editorial standard and must be framed entirely as historical record, never as an argument about any present-day political actor or party[cite: 18].

## Accuracy & Citation Policy
*   **Match to Logged Source:** Every specific number, date, name, and measurement appearing in published content must exactly match a logged source from the research package[cite: 18].
*   **Handling Academic Disputes:** Where an academic dispute exists over a specific figure, the script must either state the range with its sourcing or select the most rigorously sourced figure and note the source[cite: 18]. It must not simply select the most dramatic figure available[cite: 18].
*   **Dual Citation:** Every piece of content carries its sourcing in two places: a brief on-screen citation at the point the strongest claim is made, and a full source reference in the accompanying caption or description[cite: 18]. Sourcing is generated at the point of scripting, not reconstructed after the fact[cite: 18].
