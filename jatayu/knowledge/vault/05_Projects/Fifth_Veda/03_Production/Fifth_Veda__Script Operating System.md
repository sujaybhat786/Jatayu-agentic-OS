# Script Operating System

## Script Philosophy
A script is an argument compressed to its irreducible form, not a summary of the research package[cite: 7]. The discipline of short-form writing is strictly subtractive[cite: 12].

## Story Structure Rules
Every script operates on a fixed 4-beat short-form arc[cite: 12]:
1.  **Ultimate Hook:** Stops the scroll visually in 1.5 seconds, and verbally in 3 seconds[cite: 12].
2.  **The Body:** Delivers exactly one piece of primary-sourced evidence and one Western/modern comparison point[cite: 12]. 
3.  **The Pitfall Twist:** Undercuts the viewer's assumption via a trapdoor phrase (e.g., "But that's not even the part they hid")[cite: 12].
4.  **The Epic Ending:** One irreducible, screenshot-worthy sentence (Weapon Line) landing in total silence, followed by a silent CTA card[cite: 12].

## Evidence Integration & Body Construction
*   **One-Stat Discipline:** The Body beat carries exactly one number, one date, one named person, and one Western/modern comparison point[cite: 12]. 
*   **Constraint:** A second statistic belongs in the Pitfall Twist, never stacked into the Body[cite: 12].

## Weapon Line & Ending Philosophy
*   **Weapon Line:** The single most quotable sentence engineered to be screenshotted or repeated[cite: 7, 12]. It must be factually true, emotionally charged, and explicitly credit the Indian source[cite: 12]. It is written *before* the rest of the script[cite: 12].
*   **Epic Ending:** Delivered flat, with no hedging language ("perhaps" or "it is believed")[cite: 12]. It holds the final frame static and silent for 1–2 seconds before the CTA[cite: 12].
*   **Anti-Recap Rule:** The ending is a new frame on the topic, never a summary of what was just said[cite: 12].

## Narrative Pacing
Short-form pacing assumes a delivery rate of 155–170 words per minute[cite: 12]. 

## CTA Philosophy
The CTA is a silent on-screen text card, not a spoken line[cite: 12]. Verbal CTAs reduce short-form completion rates; the caption and final static card carry the ask[cite: 12].
